Derandomization is the task of turning a probabilistic existence result or a randomized algorithm into a deterministic, polynomial-time algorithm. When the probability of the events under consideration can be estimated by Chernoff/Hoeffding bounds (1952,1964), the Chebyshev bound or the Lovasz Local Lemma (1972), the problem has been solved (e.g. Raghavan 1988, Beck 1991, Srivastav & Stangier 1996, Fundia 1996, Srinivasan et. al 1999, Moser & Tardos, 2010).
But this has been an open problem, when the event probabilities are bounded by the famous bounded differences inequality (BDI) due to McDiarmid (1989) generalizing Hoeffding's bound via martingales. In this paper we resolve the derandomization problem for the BDI in a quite general probabilistic setting under the assumption that the conditional expectation of the objective function under consideration can be computed in polynomial time. This assumption is true, for example, whenever the objective function is a sum of partly or even fully dependent random variables with finite range. We choose two combinatorial problems in order to demonstrate its power. The first is the derandomization of a set multicover approximation algorithm (El Ouali et. al, Algorithmica 2014), where randomized rounding followed by a repairing routine is used, thus this is an inherently $2$-phase randomized algorithm causing dependencies The second application is the derandomization of the G(n,p) random graph process for the generation of deterministic graphs with a prescribed number of triangles around the expected number of triangles in G(n,p). The derandomized BDI forms a general and easy applicable frameworkfor the derandomziation of randomized algorithms, which use the BDI in its analysis.
