"Resource allocation decisions in practice often rely on expert knowledge, historical data, or machine-learned predictions. This information can improve decisions, but following it blindly can be costly. The field of algorithms with predictions has received considerable attention in recent years. In this talk, we discuss how such algorithms can use imperfect predictions while retaining provable performance guarantees when those predictions are wrong.

We focus on two settings: multi-stage online bipartite matching, where allocation decisions must be made stage by stage before future demand is revealed, and robust scheduling, where a single schedule must perform well both for a predicted scenario and in the worst case over an uncertainty set. We discuss tradeoffs between consistency with predictions and robustness to uncertainty, highlighting both the possibilities and limitations of combining these guarantees."
