A well-quasi-order (wqo) $(V,\preceq)$ is a set together with a quasi-order $\preceq$ such that for every sequence $(v_i)$ there exist $i<j$ with $v_i \preceq v_j$. Many natural quasi-orders turn out to be well-quasi-orders, one of the most prominent results in the intersection of wqo-theory and graph theory is the proof of Wagner’s Conjecture due to Robertson and Seymour. They proved that the class of undirected graphs together with the minor-relation form a wqo, which has lead to a plethora of applications and (parameterized) algorithms for well-known decision problems on minor-closed classes. From their work they derive that the class of graphs together with the weak immersion relation form another wqo.
A natural question is whether any of their results can be extended to the setting of directed graphs. Unfortunately, this turns out to be wrong for many natural minor-relations on directed graphs. On the positive side, it has been conjectured by Johnson that Eulerian directed graphs of bounded degree are well-quasi-order by a form of weak immersion.

We currently believe---and are in the process of writing it up---that we have a proof showing that this conjecture holds true, even for strong immersions. Moreover, we believe that we have a proof showing that the more general class of Eulerian directed graphs is well-quasi-ordered by weak immersion. It should be noted that our proof strategy proves a much stronger form, where, in particular, vertices are additionally allowed to be labelled from a well-quasi-order. In this talk I will report on the current state of our work, briefly present our proof strategy and provide some details on the case of Eulerian embeddable directed graphs and beyond.

This is joint work with Ken-ichi Kawarabayashi and Stephan Kreutzer
